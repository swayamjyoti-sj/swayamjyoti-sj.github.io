RWTH Training School in Artificial Intelligence & Robotics

02 / Extended academic syllabus

Robotics and AI curriculum

The supporting syllabus covers 24 modules across three progressive stages. These are reference pathways for extended training, not a claim that every module is delivered in the 14-day intensive school.

Stage 1 · Robotics foundations

8 modules · 60% practical / 40% theory · no prerequisites

  • Introduction to industrial, collaborative, mobile, humanoid, service and field robots
  • Electrical engineering foundations
  • Mechanics, mechanisms, structures and materials
  • Digital electronics, microcontrollers and embedded control
  • Computer engineering and programming
  • Networks, databases and IT systems
  • Sensors and actuators
  • Robot assembly and first-motion capstone

Stage 2 · Intermediate robotics

10 modules · 70% practical / 30% theory

  • Forward and inverse kinematics
  • Robot dynamics and motion
  • PID control and trajectory generation
  • Multi-platform robot programming
  • End effectors, tooling and manipulation
  • PLC integration and industrial automation
  • Machine vision and 2D/3D perception
  • HMI and SCADA interfaces
  • ROS 2, simulation and navigation
  • Intelligent robot integration capstone

Stage 3 · Data and AI in robotics

6 modules · 70% practical / 30% theory

  • Fundamentals of machine learning
  • Robotic systems
  • Computer Vision I
  • Ethics of AI and robotics
  • AI and data analytics for engineers
  • Robotic sensor systems

The detailed supporting syllabi describe interdisciplinary work across electrical, mechanical, electronics, computer and IT engineering. The intermediate level includes simulation tools such as Gazebo and ROS 2, vision-guided control, navigation and multi-robot integration.

03 / Applied learning

Illustrative hands-on projects

Project themes are drawn from the supporting syllabi. Actual exercises and access to hardware depend on the final programme and available facilities.

Robotic arm and pick-and-place

Integrate a manipulator, servo control, basic kinematics and a safe pick-and-place sequence.

Autonomous mobile robot

Combine sensors, motor control and obstacle avoidance, progressing to navigation and mapping.

Smart factory cell

Explore machine vision, PLC integration, Industrial IoT and automated inspection or sorting.

Intelligent collaborative robotics

Examine human–robot interaction, safety, perception and task planning.

Learning outcomes

Participants should develop a working understanding of AI and robotics terminology, experience applying selected machine-learning tools, engage with engineering use cases and present a collaborative project with technical reasoning, limitations and next steps.
