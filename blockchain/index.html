Research directions

Better evidence, not just more computation

Blockchain is not a substitute for physical validation, peer review or reproducible numerical methods. Its potential value is as a narrowly scoped integrity and provenance layer for computational science.

01 · Reproducible simulations

Publish hashes of simulation inputs, source-code versions, parameters and outputs, linking archived data to independently verifiable records.

02 · Atomistic and OFDFT workflows

Design verifiable workflows for orbital-free density functional theory, finite-element calculations and atomistic simulations without storing bulky or sensitive scientific data directly on-chain.

03 · Open benchmark challenges

Explore transparent rules for public benchmarks, independent result validation and attribution of computational contributions.

Research hypothesis

What would make this scientifically useful?

Testable question: Can a cryptographically verifiable provenance workflow improve independent auditability and traceability of computational materials results compared with ordinary signed repositories and archival records, without creating excessive cost or complexity?

Evaluation should compare overhead, reproducibility, tamper detection, metadata completeness and ease of independent verification. Where conventional methods work equally well, they should be preferred.

These are proposed research directions, not claims that a blockchain implementation or validation study has already been completed.
