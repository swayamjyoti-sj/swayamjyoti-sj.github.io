SCIENCE EDUCATION & PHILANTHROPY

Help students see how science discovers the unseen.

An invitation to foundations, educators and philanthropic partners to develop an accessible science education pilot.

Proposed initiative: from atoms to everyday materials

A workshop and illustrated learning resource showing how atomic rearrangements influence material behaviour. The initial audience would be secondary-school students, including girls considering science and engineering.

A measurable pilot

  1. Co-design with a school or education partner: age group, language, learning goals and participant needs.
  2. Create an accessible lesson, a clearly labelled simulation illustration and a teacher guide.
  3. Run a small workshop and assess understanding through short pre- and post-activities.
  4. Review student and teacher feedback, revise the resource and report the findings.

Potential partner contributions

Education expertise, a participating school, translation, venue support, resource production or pilot funding.

Budget and accountability

Agree itemized costs for preparation, delivery, accessibility and evaluation. Confirm the delivery organization, reporting responsibilities and milestones before accepting funding.

This is a proposal for discussion. A delivery partner, final budget, dates and funding arrangements have not yet been agreed.

What success would mean

Document participation, changes in understanding, teacher assessment and lessons for future delivery. Any public report would use aggregate findings and the permissions agreed with the education partner.
