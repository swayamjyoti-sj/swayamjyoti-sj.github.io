DFG PROPOSAL · INDEPENDENT VERIFICATION AND PREDICTIVE VALIDATION

Validation at every scale.

How each model is developed, calibrated on designated training data, and then evaluated on independent metallic-glass realizations and loading conditions. A successful code run is not proof of correct materials physics; a good fitted stress–strain curve is not sufficient cross-scale validation.

00 / CROSS-SCALE VALIDATION CONTRACT

Separate code verification, model calibration and independent validation

Verification

Does the implemented algorithm solve the stated equations? Check conserved quantities, dimensional consistency, refinement, Newton/ODE convergence, reverse-path closure and work/energy residuals.

Calibration

Fit or identify parameters using only preselected training glasses, reference paths or separation measurements; specify objective functions, priors or bounds, structural uncertainty and identifiability.

Validation

Without changing parameters, predict independent glass preparations, withheld loading directions, histories, temperatures, rates and fracture geometries. Compare against explicit null models with uncertainty intervals.

Ey = [ (1/Nv) Σi ((ŷi − yi)² / (si² + ε)) ]1/2

Proposal Eq. (9), displayed for traceability: si represents declared measurement or ensemble uncertainty, not a freely tuned fitting constant. Actual acceptance tolerances and sample sizes must be frozen after pilot convergence studies; none is invented here.

Freeze the data split

  • Split by entire independently prepared glass realizations, not individual frames or highly correlated events.
  • Separate held-out quench histories, loading orientations, temperatures and rates where feasible.
  • For fracture, separate calibration data from independent crack and separation tests.
  • Register model structure, feature selection, matching rules, metrics and uncertainty analysis before final tests.

Prevent leakage and false success

  • No fitting of model parameters, internal lengths or prefactors to withheld test data.
  • No reuse of near-duplicate configurations across calibration and test groups.
  • Compare spatial predictors to randomized and structure-only baselines, and mesoscale dynamics to simpler ablations.
  • Report negative results explicitly, with numerical, sampling and model errors separated.
Proposal distinction: The independent splits, falsification logic and decision gates are specified in the DFG proposal. Detailed implementation steps and candidate readouts on this webpage make them operational; they are a proposed protocol, not completed validation results.
01 / MODEL-SPECIFIC VALIDATION

Glass preparation and ARTn/NEB event catalogue · WP1

1 · Model development

  • Prepare multiple independent 3D 50:50 binary Lennard–Jones glasses; control quench history, potential, composition, cell size and relaxation tolerance.
  • Search saddles using independent ARTn starts; follow each saddle downhill to both minima; refine selected paths with NEB; deduplicate geometrically and energetically.
  • Record per-event minima, saddle, forward/reverse barriers, displacement/core geometry, transformation moment tensor and reversibility.

2 · Calibration / training

  • Choose potential and preparation protocol from documented structural/mechanical reference checks; calibrate numerical thresholds for saddle uniqueness, core matching and relaxation on development glasses only.
  • Determine required saddle-search effort, barrier-tail stability tolerances and path-matching length scale using pilot convergence; freeze those before final testing.
  • Do not tune barrier populations to reproduce the final macroscopic stress–strain curve.

3 · Independent validation

  • Hold out entire independently prepared glasses and selected quench histories. Repeat independent ARTn searches and NEB refinements.
  • Compare predicted/resampled cumulative rate weight of new unique low-barrier channels, forward/reverse path energy consistency, and escape-time quantiles.
  • Compare event-core geometry and source-state statistics across glass replicas and system sizes.
Quantitative evidenceUnique new-channel rate weight versus search effort; directional barrier consistency; overlap/duplicate error; uncertainty in escape-rate and first-passage quantiles.
Failure / decision ruleIf low-barrier rate weight or escape-time quantiles drift, do not call the network kinetically complete; carry interval-valued rates or increase sampling.
Information passed onwardOnly converged or bounded channel graph and descriptors → kinetic solver; no presumption that discovery counts equal event frequency.
Proposal basisProposal §§2.5–2.6; WP1; Gate A (M12).
02 / MODEL-SPECIFIC VALIDATION

Independent athermal stability and ARTn–AQS correspondence · WP1–WP2

1 · Model development

  • On matched inherent structures, apply directional AQS strain increments and fully minimize at each increment; monitor constrained Hessian stability.
  • Refine strain increments around the first loss of positive stability; distinguish initial bifurcation from the relaxation cascade.
  • Construct spatial event-core locations, orientations, and stress/strain thresholds without using ARTn event scores to select AQS sites.

2 · Calibration / training

  • Use development specimens to define nonaffine-core threshold, orientation comparison, spatial matching radius, loading directions and numerical strain-step refinement.
  • Pre-register randomized-site and structure-only descriptor baselines and a bootstrap confidence-interval method.
  • Freeze all thresholds before the independent comparison.

3 · Independent validation

  • Use unseen glass realizations and withheld loading directions for a blinded comparison of ARTn rate-weighted susceptibility to AQS event cores.
  • Compare spatial overlap/precision–recall, rank correlation, event orientation agreement and stress-dependent barriers against random and structure-only predictors.
  • Check dependence on preparation history, cell size and step refinement.
Quantitative evidenceOut-of-sample overlap enrichment versus controls, rank correlation, angular mismatch, first-instability strain error, and confidence intervals across independent glasses.
Failure / decision ruleIf predictive overlap is not reproducibly better than controls, report distinct thermal and athermal selection mechanisms; do not force an ARTn–AQS mapping.
Information passed onwardAQS instability thresholds and any demonstrably predictive descriptors → mesoscale; not a universal conversion of athermal threshold into an Arrhenius barrier.
Proposal basisProposal §§1.1.2, 2.3, 2.7; WP2 decisive comparison; Table 2.
03 / MODEL-SPECIFIC VALIDATION

Thermal kinetic rates and coarse-graining · WP2

1 · Model development

  • For each eligible transition, estimate kₑ(T,σ,z)=νₑ exp[−ΔG‡ₑ(T,σ,z)/(kBT)] under explicit metastability assumptions.
  • Preserve forward/reverse channels, conditional basin identities, event-rate selection and state renewal; use time-dependent hazard integration under loading.
  • Propagate uncertainty from low-barrier discovery, stress sensitivity, activation free-energy corrections and prefactors.

2 · Calibration / training

  • On designated training glasses and conditions, determine prefactor/free-energy approximations or defensible bounds; calibrate state classes and stress dependence without peeking at tests.
  • Calibrate rate-tail coverage and sensitivity to unseen channels with independent searches; fit kinetic parameters only on calibration ensembles.
  • Compare simple barrier-only, stress-conditioned and state-conditioned alternatives with a fixed calibration budget.

3 · Independent validation

  • Predict held-out event identities, first-event and residence-time distributions, relaxation curves, temperature trends and loading-rate trends.
  • Where directly accessible, compare to independent MD/first-passage trajectories on small systems; do not claim validation at unobservable timescales.
  • Check interval coverage, probability calibration and reverse-path/detailed-balance consistency where equilibrium assumptions hold.
Quantitative evidenceHeld-out waiting-time quantile error; distributional distance; event-choice log score/calibration; confidence/coverage of interval-valued predictions.
Failure / decision ruleIf held-out kinetics systematically fall outside predicted uncertainty bands, reject unqualified rate transfer or add explicit state classes; preserve uncertainty.
Information passed onwardConditional hazards, reverse transitions, transformation moments and uncertainty → stochastic mesoscale solver; not arithmetic mean barriers, artificial seconds or raw ARTn discovery frequencies.
Proposal basisProposal §§2.6–2.7; WP2; H1; Gate B (M20).
04 / MODEL-SPECIFIC VALIDATION

Interacting-event stochastic elastoplasticity · WP3

1 · Model development

  • Assign state-dependent event channels, tensorial transformation moments and region-specific local stresses to mesoscopic cells.
  • Implement controlled ablations: A independent rate-weighted events; B the same with elastic stress redistribution; C B plus atomistically informed structural renewal.
  • Compute stress equilibration, event-triggered correlations, avalanches and finite-duration spatial localization.

2 · Calibration / training

  • Fit spatial coarse-graining length, elastic kernel/boundary implementation, state renewal parameters and event-moment distributions on the calibration group only.
  • Use training trajectories/stress drops/accessible kinetic observables; document exactly which quantities came from atomistic data versus fitted closures.
  • Keep model A/B/C initial states, temperatures, driving, discretization, calibration information and uncertainty budgets comparable.

3 · Independent validation

  • On independent glass preparations and held-out temperatures, shear directions and strain rates, predict stress-relaxation curves, stress drops and avalanche occurrence.
  • Compare event-event correlation length, cluster-size distributions, cutoff identifiability, cascade probability and shear-band initiation, orientation and persistence.
  • Run randomized interactions and no-renewal controls; bootstrap by independent glass, not by correlated frames.
Quantitative evidenceHeld-out relaxation and stress-drop errors, avalanche probability with intervals, cutoff sensitivity across sizes, correlation length and finite-time band-persistence metrics.
Failure / decision ruleIf interactions/renewal do not improve prediction beyond ensemble scatter, report the null result. Audit catalogue incompleteness, prefactors and finite-size bias before interpreting physical failure.
Information passed onwardMesoscale stress–strain histories, stress fluctuations, internal state variables, dissipation and correlation scales → continuum RVE; not atom-by-atom paths or an automatic fracture law.
Proposal basisProposal WP3, §§2.8–2.9 and three-model ablation.
05 / MODEL-SPECIFIC VALIDATION

Finite-element RVE and constitutive homogenization · WP4

1 · Model development

  • Solve mesoscale RVEs with periodic and independently selected admissible alternative boundary conditions; implement first Piola stress/deformation gradient work conjugacy.
  • Verify Hill–Mandel work identity and separate elastic energy storage from plastic dissipation; calculate algorithmic tangents and evolving state variables.
  • Compare deterministic local, stochastic, memory-dependent and nonlocal/gradient continuum closures; test localization with mesh and length-scale refinement.

2 · Calibration / training

  • On calibration RVEs, estimate continuum parameters/length scales using microstructural response, work-conjugate stress, state histories and independently specified regularization.
  • Select RVE size and boundary-condition handling by pre-defined size-convergence and energy tests. Set constitutive solver tolerances on pilots.
  • Freeze continuum parameters and internal lengths before withheld load paths; never re-tune them to achieve better test stress–strain agreement.

3 · Independent validation

  • Predict withheld 3D loading paths, strain rates, temperatures and boundary conditions without recalibration.
  • Check Hill–Mandel residual and energy/dissipation budgets separately from statistical self-averaging; compute RVE mean/variance and spatial-correlation convergence.
  • Check finite-difference tangent consistency, Newton/step refinement, mesh-objective band onset, width and orientation, and history dependence.
Quantitative evidenceNormalized Hill–Mandel residual; energy/dissipation residual; tangent error; RVE mean/variance vs size; held-out stress/path error; mesh-sensitivity of localization metrics.
Failure / decision ruleIf no representative volume exists or localized response is non-self-averaging, restrict deterministic local closure to convergent regimes; retain stochastic/nonlocal alternatives and map validity limits.
Information passed onwardVerified stress P(F,state), consistent tangent, calibrated internal lengths, dissipation and uncertainty → continuum boundary-value problem. No claim that shear localization directly specifies decohesion.
Proposal basisProposal WP4, §2.9; H3; Gate C (M27).
06 / MODEL-SPECIFIC VALIDATION

Conditional material damage, fracture and decohesion · WP4–WP5

1 · Model development

  • Separate plastic-strain localization from cohesive damage. Introduce a physically grounded cohesive-zone or phase-field fracture model with explicit irreversibility and finite separation work.
  • Solve incremental equilibrium together with fracture evolution; monitor energy partition into elastic, plastic and fracture contributions.
  • Test crack-initiation, propagation/arrest and mixed-mode sensitivity only when input separation physics can be grounded independently.

2 · Calibration / training

  • Obtain cohesive strength, Mode-I/II separation law or fracture energy Gc, initiation observable and damage length scale from independent crack/separation datasets or explicitly validated atomistic separation tests.
  • Use separate calibration specimens and crack geometries; do not back-infer Gc from LSE activation barriers, shear-band width or global plastic stress response alone.
  • Freeze damage parameters and fracture regularization before crack tests.

3 · Independent validation

  • Predict held-out crack initiation load/location, growth/arrest, crack trajectory, traction–opening response and independent separation work.
  • Check mesh/phase-field length scale convergence, energy balance, damage irreversibility, size dependence and path predictivity under alternative loading.
  • Compare fracture predictions with non-fracture plastic localization outcomes to detect false positives.
Quantitative evidenceInitiation load/strain error; crack-path distance or overlap; toughness/separation-work error; mesh/length sensitivity; damage and total-energy residual.
Failure / decision ruleWithout independently supplied separation data, label figures and solvers as methodological demonstrations—not validated metallic-glass fracture predictions; stop at verified localization physics.
Information passed onwardDamage/fracture predictions only with independent cohesive data and validated continuum fields; neither 'LSE → crack' nor 'shear band → material separation' is an automatic map.
Proposal basisProposal §1.1.4; WP4 conditional extension; WP5; Gate D (M33).
07 / COMPUTING AND DELIVERY

How are calibration and validation funded within the HPC request?

The DFG proposal provides a provisional 1,000,000 CPU-core-hour envelope: 150,000 for preparation/equilibration, 600,000 for ARTn/NEB, 150,000 jointly for AQS and replica validation, and 100,000 for mesoscale/kinetic sensitivity. These are workload totals, not validation-only money or a confirmed allocation. Every model also needs convergence and independent tests within its own workload.

ActivityExisting envelopeWhere validation costs occur
Preparation / equilibration150,000 core-hAdditional independent held-out glasses; preparation-history and cell-size checks
ARTn / NEB600,000 core-hRepeated independent saddle searches, reverse pathways, low-tail convergence and path checks
AQS and replica tests150,000 core-hDirectional instabilities, independent event-core comparisons and numerical refinement; not 150,000 solely for validation
Mesoscale / kinetics100,000 core-hCalibration, ablations, unseen loading conditions, closure transfer tests and uncertainty sensitivity

The Computing Resources subpage separately shows an illustrative, not yet adopted redistribution of the final 100,000 core-hours into 60,000 mesoscale, 25,000 continuum FE and 15,000 conditional cohesive fracture. Because these activities also require independent validation, this is a planning subdivision—not a second or additional validation budget. A defensible internal development-versus-validation division will follow pilot runtime measurements; no arbitrary share is asserted as approved.
