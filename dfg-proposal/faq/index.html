01 / 10 QUESTIONS

Fundamental science, novelty and research strategy

DFG fundamental questions, potential discoveries, scientific impact, feasibility and research risks.

1.What fundamental science does this project develop, which is something that DFG looks at?

The fundamental questions are whether thermal activated events predict athermal instabilities; which rare low-barrier channels dominate physical time; how local event interactions produce avalanches and shear bands; when a stochastic, history-dependent process admits a continuum closure; and why plastic localization does or does not imply cracking. These are falsifiable mechanics, statistical-physics and nonequilibrium questions, not an engineering product plan. The DFG explicitly funds knowledge-driven research and considers scientific quality and originality central. The project should lead with hypotheses, competing explanations, independent tests and valuable negative outcomes rather than promises of applications.

2.What discoveries can we await in the proiect?

Potential outcomes include: a measured link—or falsified link—between thermal LSE susceptibility and AQS instability; state- and orientation-dependent event-rate laws; the degree to which elastic interactions and renewal cause avalanches; kinetic freezing and shear-band onset regimes; measurable domains where local constitutive laws self-average; and limits of predicting cracks from plastic localization. These are research hypotheses, not assured discoveries. A strong negative result with uncertainty bounds remains a valuable advance.

3.What significant advance will this research project lead to, and how valuable will that be for the glass community?

The high-value contribution would be a verified chain from atomistic transition networks to kinetic event selection and collective plasticity, including criteria for retaining event identity, orientation, rate distributions and memory. If it succeeds, it offers reusable benchmarks and validated closure domains; if it fails, it identifies mechanisms that cannot be safely averaged. Either outcome narrows a major open gap between glassy relaxation and deformation, especially when accompanied by open code and independent tests.

4.If we fully solve the multiscale model that connects atomistic to fracture in metallic glasses, what would be the impact?

A validated atomistic-to-fracture predictive chain would be a major scientific advance if it predicts, on unseen glasses and loading paths, rates, collective yielding, localization, crack initiation and propagation while transporting uncertainty and dissipation correctly. It could reduce empirical constitutive fitting and suggest alloy-specific toughening mechanisms. However, “fully solved” must mean a specified class of alloys, temperature/strain-rate/loading conditions and calibrated separation physics; no finite model becomes universally valid for every glass. The strongest claim would be quantitative transferability with clearly measured failure boundaries and open replication artifacts.

5.In the era of AI, will this project actually take 3 years, or can it be done faster?

AI can accelerate coding, saddle-search scheduling, surrogate modelling and literature/data organization, but it cannot supply independent rare-event convergence, unbiased test glasses, physical time mapping, fracture calibration or reproducibility on demand. Some pilot modules can be built early. Thirty-six months remains defensible for cross-scale falsification and publication if work packages overlap and strict go/no-go gates prevent unbounded scope. Report faster completion only if validation is genuinely satisfied, not because illustrative examples already run.

6.What directions in this project, can be repeated research, and how to avoid them?

Repeating known ARTn morphology or a basic AQS stress–strain plot without a new test would add little; likewise generic STZ population curves, a prescribed FE constitutive law, or illustrative fracture with uncalibrated Gc are not novel. Avoid repetition through a prior-work matrix, preregistered discriminating hypotheses, independent baselines, held-out matched ARTn–AQS tests, rate-tail convergence, and work/kinetic validation. Define novelty as a demonstrably new verified connection or a robust identified failure.

7.Peter Derlet would tell me (Swayamjyoti) a building a fully multiscale model is difficult and time-taking and will be hard to generalize to all materials, due to time-scale problems of MD, and other small bottlenecks that we find during upscaling? How to manage that?

Treat that as the central risk. Never promise one universal all-materials bridge. Prioritize one LJ reference plus at most one selected alloy; model only observable, kinetically active low-barrier channels, with bounds on unseen channels and free-energy/prefactor uncertainty. Use hybrid thermal KMC + athermal AQS with an explicit crossover, compare finite-size controls, and make continuum closure conditional on RVE self-averaging. Use quarterly decision gates, computational profiling, validation splits and credible fallbacks; report the bounded predictive domain as the result.

8.How can this project help with problems in studies of glass transition?

It can connect microscopic activated networks, dynamical heterogeneity, kinetic freezing, aging and structural memory to observables near and below the glass transition. Track temperature-dependent waiting-time distributions, persistence, structural relaxation functions and spatial correlations. Yet a proposal focused on deformation and metallic glasses cannot claim to solve the fundamental thermodynamic glass transition; a strong contribution is testing how local barriers and collective cascades shape relaxation.

9.How does this project connect with research on disorder materials?

It offers a transferable design of hypotheses—local softness versus activation, landscape topology, rare-event kinetics, memory, avalanches, and breakdown of self-averaging—relevant to oxide glasses, polymers, colloids, granular matter and some disordered biological/mechanical networks. Force fields, interactions, conservation laws and deformation mechanisms are material specific, so generalize algorithms and validation criteria first rather than assuming one parameter set.

10.What can I present at 2027 Nobel Prize Teacher Summit in Stockholm, which I am attending, that will inspire high school teachers for metallic glasses?

An engaging teacher-focused module could be ‘Why does a metal behave like glass, and why does one atomic rearrangement sometimes cause failure?’ Use bead models for crystalline versus disordered packing, animated reversible ring/chain LSEs, two contrasting ARTn–AQS cases and an avalanche demo. Let teachers predict what happens when the load reverses; then connect probability, energy landscapes, experiments and reproducibility. Provide a low-cost classroom kit and clear distinctions between established results and hypotheses. Do not describe an unconfirmed speaking slot as official.

Atomistic LSEs, STZs, activation barriers and relaxation

What an event means, how to compare ARTn with AQS, low-barrier tails, α/β relaxation and the free-volume question.

11.Give a zoomed in colorful figure where at the atomistic scale on ARTn LSE is the same AQS STZ, give me another figure where it's not, explain all hypothetical situations?

The same physical rearrangement is possible when a thermally accessible ARTn saddle connects the same basins as the AQS instability under the same loading direction. It is NOT guaranteed. Possible mismatches: (i) a thermal transition is reversible while AQS selects irreversible plasticity; (ii) an ARTn saddle exists but stress destabilizes a different mode; (iii) the same core transforms with different orientation or final state; (iv) ARTn and AQS share a core but differ in which event starts a cascade; (v) thermal and athermal thresholds coincide only near a fold. Use same initial glass, align strain and cell, map displaced atoms/core overlap, eigenstrain orientation, saddle endpoints and Hessian instability, with randomized controls. 13.How does the ARTn LSE and AQS STZ, connect with the terminology from the undercooled liquids community, such as β-relaxation and α-relaxation?

LSE and STZ describe local microscopic rearrangements or shear-transformable configurations, while α- and β-relaxation classify collective and secondary relaxation processes by timescale, length scale and response. A localized, frequently reversible activated LSE may contribute to secondary (Johari–Goldstein-type) β dynamics, but not every LSE equals a β event. Many correlated local events may collectively relax structural memory on α timescales; an athermal STZ is not automatically α or β. Measure temperature-dependent self-intermediate scattering, persistence, dynamic susceptibility and transition networks before assigning relaxation labels.

14.What are LSEs, are these β events or are these α events?

LSE means a localized transition/pathway between inherent structures, often chain-like/ring-like. The label says geometry/path kinetics, NOT which relaxation peak it contributes to. Small repeated/reversible events may be candidates for β contributions; a sequence of coupled LSEs can contribute to α structural relaxation. Use measured relaxation-spectrum participation and dynamic correlations to classify, and allow overlapping or neither category.

15.What are STZs, are these β events or are these α events?

An STZ is a shear-transformable local configuration or a plastic rearrangement activated mechanically/thermally in a constitutive interpretation. Its classification as α or β is not intrinsic. A local reversible transformation could contribute secondary relaxation; collective activated STZ networks under finite temperature can contribute to structural α relaxation, while AQS events at T≈0 are not thermal relaxation peaks.

16.How to climb up the α hill via collective β activity? Can we climb again from the final minimum, is there are smarter way to reach the top of the α hill?

Replace the metaphor with a tested multi-basin pathway search: from initial minimum A, use ARTn to enumerate local low-barrier connections; after reaching B, run ARTn again and grow a directed graph. Search for a low-effective-cost multi-step route A→B→C… via a priority queue/transition-path sampling/kinetic Monte Carlo, accounting for reverse rates and recrossings. The α process may be a connected network, not one giant saddle atop a single hill. A 'smart' target is first-passage kinetics and committor, not merely maximum potential energy.

17.If we run ARTn from multiple local minima, which are initial minima, will the result in Swayamjyoti's 2nd PRB that connects strained LSE correlation to far field shear in XY direction depicting linear correlation, still hold, or do you think that, there will be no correlation?

A near-linear first-order barrier shift may survive WITHIN a given basin and small-strain regime, where ΔG‡(γ)≈ΔG‡(0)−γV‡:∂σ/∂γ. Across multiple initial minima, activation tensors, orientations, residual stresses and basin identities vary; pooling them can weaken, reverse or hide the relationship (even produce a Simpson's-paradox effect). Fit per-basin slopes and hierarchical between-basin distributions, test nonlinearities near instabilities, include pressure/shear orientation and hold out whole minima. Do not extrapolate a single-basin correlation unchanged.

18.The tail of the activation barrier distribution is the physically relevant stat, how does that change the entire multiscale model?

A crucial correction is that the entire barrier distribution is not equally important for kinetics: the total escape hazard is K(T,σ,z)=Σ_e ν_e exp[−ΔG‡_e(T,σ,z)/(kBT)]. At a given temperature, unusually low barriers can dominate, but the relevant quantity is the prefactor- and multiplicity-weighted low-barrier tail, plus channel connectivity and revisits—not simply the minimum barrier or the raw low-energy count. This changes every stage: ARTn search must target uncertainty in cumulative rate weight; kinetics must propagate missing-channel bounds; mesoscale models must sample hazard-weighted event orientations and spatial correlations; continuum closure must retain rare-trigger fluctuations if self-averaging fails. Report the cumulative fraction of rate carried below barrier b, the effective number of active channels, and escape-time uncertainty versus catalogue effort. At higher T many channels may matter; under strain the tail itself changes. An extreme-value-only fit could be badly biased if search and sampling are incomplete.

19.How did Peter Derlet first get the evidence of thermally activated events at the atomistic scale, what's the experimental evidence for it, and how does that change the entire STZ-based theories?

Evidence comes from multiple lines of work rather than one unique first observation. Derlet and collaborators used long-time molecular dynamics showing local structural excitations during annealing and stress relaxation, including string-like motion, internal-stress change and temperature dependence (thermal processing MD; transport/microplasticity on microsecond timescales). Experiments on metallic glasses independently show relaxation spectra, aging/enthalpy recovery, rate- and temperature-dependent mechanical response and intermittent dynamics; those are consistent with activated events but often cannot identify an individual ARTn saddle. A physically founded STZ model should include distributed barriers, stress/orientation-dependent activation, reversible transitions and state renewal; conventional phenomenological STZ models need not be discarded. The key new test is whether a measured event network predicts independent macroscopic kinetics better.

20.When Frans Spaepen visited ETH Zurich, he told me, that my LSEs look like they are moving towards regions of free volume. This is a parallel with his early free volume theories. How do I establish that?

Treat the recollected discussion as a hypothesis to test, not proof. For each ARTn transition define an event-core centroid and displacement/transition direction, then compute three-dimensional free-volume descriptors around the initial minimum (Voronoi volumes with species-aware radii; local packing; a cavity or insertion-probability map). Compare the directional projection Δr_core·∇v_free with zero using rotated-event, shuffled-site and density-matched controls. Check whether the event travels toward high available volume, creates a void while moving, or whether both reflect an underlying soft region. Condition on composition, hydrostatic stress, barrier and event size; test across independent minima and glasses. A flux or spatial cross-correlation versus time/path coordinate distinguishes cause from consequence. Compare explanatory power to Spaepen-style free-volume kinetics and retain uncertainty when descriptors disagree.

21.How does this project connect with Frans Spaepen's free volume theories?

Spaepen-type free-volume perspectives propose that local available volume influences activated flow. The thesis found correlations with atomic volume/free volume that were limited rather than deterministic. Test free-volume descriptors against chemistry, local modulus, rate-weighted barrier, quasivoid and graph predictors using partial correlations and held-out glasses. Distinguish thermodynamic volume, geometrical Voronoi volume and fragmented available volume: they are not interchangeable.

22.Why testing reversibility of LSEs are important?

Reversibility distinguishes recoverable anelastic excitations from permanent plastic flow. For a minimum–saddle–minimum A→S→B, independently search B→A; check reverse barriers, atomic recovery, transformation moments and local stress. Under cyclic positive and negative shear, record return to A versus transition onward to C, residual strain and energy dissipation. A reverse path may exist yet be too slow at the relevant temperature. Separate reversible switching, biased metastability, and cascade-triggered irreversible events in the kinetic model; validate rates and statistical uncertainty across independent glasses. Do not label all reversible events as β relaxations.

23.Why are reverse activation barriers important in LSEs?

An ARTn local structural excitation (LSE) is a directed transition between two inherent-structure minima, A and B, through a saddle S. The forward and reverse barriers are ΔEA→B=ES−EA and ΔEB→A=ES−EB. They are generally unequal if the two minima have different energies. Their difference obeys ΔEA→B−ΔEB→A=EB−EA, providing an important path/energy consistency check.

Physical interpretation: a low forward barrier alone does not tell us whether an event produces lasting plastic rearrangement. If the reverse transition is fast under the relevant temperature and stress, the atomic rearrangement may repeatedly switch A↔B, producing anelastic recovery or secondary relaxation without accumulating permanent plastic strain. If the return transition is slow or a subsequent cascade changes the basin network, the event may contribute to aging, structural renewal or irreversible plasticity. Existence of a reverse path does not mean an experimentally observable reverse jump will occur within the loading timescale.

Kinetic significance: both directed rates should be carried into the network, for example kA→B≈νABexp[−ΔG‡AB/(kBT)] and kB→A≈νBAexp[−ΔG‡BA/(kBT)]. For an equilibrium two-state model with consistent free energies and prefactors, their ratio must reproduce the correct equilibrium populations (detailed balance). Stress can change each directional barrier differently because the forward and reverse activation volumes need not coincide. Failing to represent return hops can overcount net plastic strain and distort waiting times and dissipation.

How to test in the DFG project: (1) start ARTn from both A and B and verify the same saddle connectivity, minima, and reverse path using relaxation/NEB; (2) compare barriers and energy identity at tighter tolerances; (3) measure or bound directional prefactors and rates across temperature and applied shear; (4) perform cyclic AQS or driven finite-temperature simulations to detect actual recovery versus renewed rearrangements; (5) pass reversible and irreversible branches separately to the mesoscale solver; (6) validate predicted recoverable strain, hysteresis, relaxation times and residual plastic strain on held-out glass samples. Reverse barriers are thus essential for physically defensible kinetic coarse-graining, not simply an extra catalogue column.

Important limitation: barrier height alone is insufficient to decide α versus β relaxation or irreversible fracture; collective connectivity, structural memory, event orientation, prefactors and observation timescale all matter.

24.What are states in LSEs and STZs?

An ARTn state is an inherent-structure minimum/basin with specified coordinates, cell and boundaries, linked to another state by a saddle. At finite temperature a metastable basin may encompass many nearby minima. An STZ model instead uses coarse states such as shear-oriented configurations, transformable populations, local stress and structural memory variables. To connect them, map directed atomistic events, their moment tensors and forward/reverse rates into STZ states, then test whether all atomistic states assigned the same label exhibit comparable future hazards. Failure implies extra memory or additional state variables.

25.How can we measure LSEs and/or STZs experimentally?

Most atomistic saddles cannot be observed directly. Seek their signatures through mechanical spectroscopy and secondary relaxation, creep, stress relaxation, temperature/rate-dependent yielding, intermittent nanoindentation or micropillar loading, X-ray photon correlation spectroscopy and TEM/4D-STEM structural imaging. Compare measured activation volumes, strain bursts, dynamic correlations and relaxation spectra with simulations under matched preparation/loading. Different mechanisms can explain similar observables: no single stress drop or microscopy feature proves an individual ARTn LSE. See Derlet, Bocquet and Maaß.

26.If we investigate LSEs in glasses via femtosecond lasers what can we find?

Femtosecond pump–probe and ultrafast diffraction can resolve electronic and phonon response and potentially a transient structural pathway; post-pulse relaxation and mechanical spectroscopy assess persistent rejuvenation or altered β-like motion. Study fluence, pulse number and delay, with controls for heating, shocks, melting and damage. PRB 2024 reports laser-shock-induced changes in fast relaxation and medium-range order in a metallic glass. A femtosecond excitation does not mean the thermally activated waiting time of an LSE is femtoseconds or that a single saddle has been directly imaged.

03 / 6 QUESTIONS

Statistical mechanics, theories and multiscale methods

Derlet, Spaepen, Lam, Parisi, variational homogenization and physical model alternatives.

27.How compatible will be Peter Derlet's thermally activated models with Linder and Miehe's upscaling methods?

Complementary but not plug-and-play. Derlet-style activated networks provide stress-dependent rates, transition strains and structural-memory statistics. Miehe-style variational homogenization provides work-conjugate stresses and thermodynamically consistent incremental response; Linder-related embedded-discontinuity techniques can represent localized deformation. The bridge must carry state evolution and dissipation. A rate-dependent Markov process may not admit one local, deterministic incremental potential, especially during correlated avalanches. Test Hill–Mandel separately from rate preservation and RVE self-averaging. Miehe homogenization; gradient plasticity.

28.Is upscaling via homogenization techniques of Christian Miehe and Christian Linder is sound, or is it just because Swayamjyoti was a student with them? What are the alternatives?

Method selection must be evidence-based and independent of academic relationships. Miehe-type finite-strain incremental variational homogenization has a rigorous work-consistency foundation, while Linder-associated localization formulations are computational representations of bands, not a proof of metallic-glass kinetic transfer. Alternatives include direct atomistic-to-continuum concurrent coupling (QC/bridging domain), FE² with stateful stochastic RVEs, kinetic Monte Carlo plus Eshelby elastoplastic lattices, STZ/effective-temperature PDEs, nonlocal/gradient plasticity, peridynamics, phase-field and data-driven constitutive operators. Compare all on identical calibration and held-out tests, cost and work/dissipation criteria.

29.How will this project connect with Prof. Lam (Hong Kong)'s Quasi-void theories?

Chi-Hang Lam's quasivoid and distinguishable-particle lattice/configuration-tree ideas provide a competing localized-defect description of string-like relaxation. Compare quasivoid occupancy/migration with ARTn string cores, free-volume fragments and rate-weighted excitations on the SAME configurations. Your coauthored quasivoid paper offers a direct starting point, and Lam-linked colloidal observations show quasi-void transport can cause strings. An observed string is not by itself proof of a unique quasivoid.

30.How will this project connect with Prof. Normand (Canada, pioneer of ARTn technique)'s research?

The most direct collaboration is to make ARTn event catalogues genuinely kinetically trustworthy: saddle searches, geometric duplicate tests, minima connectivity, low-barrier completeness, stress-dependent variants, barrier uncertainty and efficient rare-event selection. Compare modern ARTn, selected NEB refinements and direct small-system dynamics. The contribution would go beyond finding LSEs toward statistically defensible rate-preserving transfer and falsification against AQS.

31.How does this project connect with Parisi's work on spin glasses?

The conceptual link is rugged landscapes, frustration, many metastable states, state overlap distributions, aging and non-self-averaging. One can compute overlaps among glass configurations, measure barrier-network heterogeneity and compare history-dependent escape statistics to mean-field glass insights. Parisi's replica-symmetry-breaking theory for spin glasses is not a ready-made constitutive model for atomistic metallic glasses; the connection requires careful observables and should avoid claiming the same universality class. Nobel scientific context.

32.What's the best way to upscale Derlet-Maass thermally activated model, from mesoscale to continuum scale?

Start with a stochastic 3D mesoscale representative volume retaining stress-dependent event hazards, reversible transitions, eigenstrain moments, elastic interactions and structural renewal. Impose deformation-gradient/temperature histories and record average first-Piola stress, work, consistent tangent, fluctuations and correlation scales. Verify Hill–Mandel equality, boundary and size convergence, then calibrate a finite-strain history-dependent constitutive operator only if self-averaging holds. If shear-band avalanches remain nonlocal, retain stochastic/gradient/nonlocal closure. Fracture energy requires separate calibration. Derlet–Maaß-related model results motivate these tests, not a universal deterministic closure.

Electronic structure, thermal coupling, AI and interdisciplinary methods

Orbital-free DFT, AI, DeepMind, AlphaFold and cross-disciplinary inverse problems.

33.Apart from mechanical scale bridging, can we do a thermal scale bridging at all levels?

Yes, as an additional scope: atomistic vibrational and activated free-energy contributions → local heat production and thermal conductivity → mesoscale thermally coupled event dynamics → continuum heat equation plus inelastic dissipation. One candidate local balance is ρc Ṫ = ∇·(k∇T) + χ τ:ε̇p + q_external, with χ and k independently calibrated. The thesis mainly supports mechanical barriers and strain sensitivity, not validated heat conductivities, heat capacities or thermomechanical coupling. The full thermal programme could exceed the core 36-month scope; start with isothermal kinetic-temperature sweeps, then test a small coupled benchmark.

34.How can real space DFT methods by Vikram Gavini's group be linked? And, if investigating in that direction will be interesting?

Real-space finite-element DFT, including the DFT-FE code, could benchmark a small subset of model-alloy structures, forces, elastic constants, stress responses, and local saddle chemistry. This helps calibrate/validate classical metallic-alloy potentials and connect bond/electronic descriptors to barriers. Direct quantum ARTn across thousands of large glass configurations would be infeasible; use small representative motifs plus active learning. It is interesting for a focused, separately resourced collaboration, not a required production method.

35.If I implement my (Swayamjyoti's master thesis) on FE based Orbital Free DFT with Christian Linder, will that be an interesting paper, or will that be of not that high impact? Can we get something interesting out of it?

A straight reimplementation of the master's FE orbital-free DFT formulation is mainly a numerical validation/teaching contribution, because real-space nonperiodic FE-OFDFT and quasi-continuum OFDFT were published by Gavini and colleagues long ago. Higher scientific value requires a new benchmark or method: adaptive electronic/atomic FE meshes near a disordered shear-transformation core, a robust orbital-free kinetic functional for a selected metal, variational coupling of electron density to mechanical transition paths, or DFT-informed local barrier corrections benchmarked against Kohn–Sham DFT. DFT-FE is Kohn–Sham, not an orbital-free solver; compare honestly. Start with a small representative metallic motif and require measurable accuracy, scaling or novel physics before making it a paper.

36.How can Artificial Intelligence usage lead to significant discoveries in this research project?

Best uses are rare-event prioritization, uncertainty-aware active learning of new saddles, graph models predicting directional susceptibility, learned but physically constrained event-rate surrogates, sensitivity analysis and identifying failures of transfer. Train by independent glass, not atom/frame; compare to structural baselines and require novel interpretable descriptors to survive held-out AQS directions. Generative models can propose candidate paths, but every proposed saddle must be re-minimized and Hessian/NEB validated. AI should reduce wasted searches rather than replace conservation and energy checks.

37.How does this project link to Google DeepMind's research projects on glasses and/or disordered materials?

Direct relevant precedent: Google DeepMind's glassy-dynamics graph neural network predicts dynamical propensity from particle positions, and code/data have been released in its glassy_dynamics research repository. The DFG question extends structural prediction to saddle-resolved barrier kinetics, directional AQS, physical rates and continuum scale transfer. An informative benchmark is GNN-predicted event susceptibility versus rate-weighted ARTn versus structure-only features on unseen preparations; no collaboration is implied.

38.How can this project connect with AlphaFold by Google DeepMind, and if there are any transferable information, which can be used by either side?

Transferable methods include equivariant geometric features, uncertainty calibration, learned representations of local environments, structural ensembles and physics-informed generative proposals. AlphaFold's training targets are biomolecular structures/interactions—not activated transition rates in amorphous metals. A proof-of-concept would compare equivariant graph representations on held-out atomistic LSE descriptors; any reverse transfer to proteins would require independent biomolecular tasks and data. AlphaFold 3.

39.How can this project connect with Swayamjyoti's recent research paper with Donna Strickland, https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02463?

The cited numerically optimized FROG paper uses an inverse reconstruction method (including Adam optimization) to recover optical fields from measurements. The link is methodology, not a shared physical mechanism: constrained inverse problems, noisy measurement fitting, identifiability, uncertainty, regularization, adaptive optimization and held-out verification. In metallic glasses analogous inverse tasks reconstruct rate distributions or local constitutive parameters from atomistic/mesoscale observables. Optical frequency retrieval should not be presented as direct evidence for glass plasticity.

40.How can this project contribute to the advanced of Artificial Intelligence and other popular and emerging technologies?

A strong AI-methods contribution would be a rare-event benchmark where models must predict unseen saddles, activation barriers, transitions and uncertainties across independently prepared glasses, not just visually attractive atomistic configurations. Relevant transferable methods include equivariant graph representations, active learning for rare tails, physically constrained kinetic operators, uncertainty calibration, differentiable inverse modelling and scientific foundation-model evaluation. For robotics/space systems the mechanics output could support material lifetime assessment; for digital twins it provides uncertainty-aware constitutive state models. These are prospective contributions and require open datasets, baseline comparisons and independent benchmarks; they do not establish that one glass-trained model generalizes to arbitrary materials.

41.How can this project connect with Joachim Frank's potential energy landscapes in his research?

Joachim Frank's cryo-EM work maps molecular conformational heterogeneity and conformational space, which is conceptually analogous to identifying distinct basins and pathways. A useful methodological exchange concerns state discovery, low-dimensional manifold reconstruction, uncertainty and pathway kinetics. Cryo-EM particle-image populations do not directly measure metallic-glass saddle barriers, so do not equate the empirical ensembles. Frank, mapping conformational space.

42.What does the implication of Navier Stokes smoothness and existence problem, which OpenAI have claimed to have solved?

OpenAI announced on 8 September 2026 a claimed finite-time singularity in forced three-dimensional incompressible Navier–Stokes, with a Lean formalization; Nature reported the claim. Independent acceptance and correspondence to the exact Millennium Prize formulation should be distinguished from the announcement. This does not solve metallic-glass continuum equations: it motivates attention to well-posedness, loss of ellipticity in strain localization, scale-dependent regularization, numerical verification and rigorous AI-assisted mathematical discovery. It supplies no direct ARTn-to-continuum constitutive law.

43.How does the atomistic AMP code developed at Brown university, by Peterson can help in this project?

Amp (Atomistic Machine-learning Package), developed by Andrew Peterson and collaborators at Brown, is a trainable energy/force model with ASE integration. Fit reference atomistic data, explicitly including high-strain and saddle-neighborhood states, to accelerate candidate alloy ARTn/NEB searches. Validate energies, forces, Hessian modes, transformation stresses and barrier rankings on independent structures; use active learning around uncertain rare paths. A model trained only near minima may badly extrapolate at saddles. Compare with modern equivariant potentials before committing production HPC.

05 / 4 QUESTIONS

Material specificity, finite-size effects and glass families

Metallic versus oxide and spin glasses, sample size, realistic potentials and application limitations.

44.How would these multiscale models vary between metallic glass, silica glass, window glass or even spin glasses?

Metallic glasses: dense metallic coordination, composition-dependent many-body interactions, local plastic transformations and ductile/brittle shear bands. Silica: directional Si–O network, bond-switching and topology-constrained rearrangements, often different fracture and pressure sensitivity. Window glass: usually multicomponent soda–lime–silica with modifiers, alkali mobility, environmental stress corrosion and surface-flaw-dominated fracture; it is not interchangeable with pure silica. Spin glasses: frustrated magnetic degrees of freedom with energy-landscape trapping and aging but generally no atomic displacement-based STZ or mechanical crack law. Transfer the mathematical framework (basin networks, rare-event kinetics, memory, coarse-graining and uncertainty), not the same potential, event type or constitutive parameters; replace the state variables and experimental observables for each material.

45.Will using the bigger samples (in Swayamjyoti's PhD thesis (upto 1 million atoms), lead to better match with experiments, or will the qualitative trends remain the same?

Larger samples improve spatial correlation and avalanche/band statistics, suppress some finite-size artifacts and may capture rare-event low-tail extremes differently. But a larger classical LJ cell is not automatically more experimentally realistic: potential chemistry, cooling rate, length/time mapping, loading boundary conditions and thickness matter. Use a size ladder with multiple independent glasses, finite-size scaling, matched strain protocols and uncertainty. Qualitative morphology may persist while barrier tails, avalanche cutoffs and localization thresholds change. Thesis preparation at large N does not mean complete ARTn catalogues at N≈10⁶.

46.Will using more sophisticated potentials that compared to Swayamjyoti's PhD thesis give better results, or better match with experiments?

Possibly, if an alloy-specific embedded-atom, machine-learned or DFT-informed potential better reproduces the target alloy's structure, elastic response, relative barriers and stress/temperature trends on independent tests. Complexity alone does not guarantee transfer; large potentials can be poorly calibrated at saddles or fracture surfaces. Start with transparent LJ benchmark then a selected realistic alloy, document uncertainty and evaluate barriers, stress drops and deformation against experiments/DFT.

47.Can metallic glasses help with optical communication in undersea cables?

Submarine optical transmission uses low-loss silica fibers; bulk metallic glass is generally absorbing at telecom wavelengths and is not a credible silica-core replacement. Plausible auxiliary uses are precision connector or alignment mechanisms, high-pressure housings, sealed actuators and wear-resistant supports, subject to corrosion, fatigue and cost tests. Nonmetallic chalcogenide photonic glasses are a different material class. Your DFG work could help establish failure reliability of a selected metallic-glass support component, not lower optical attenuation in the transmission fiber.
