Numerical Scale Bridging.

One connected and fully traceable numerical example: atomic saddle barriers → continuous-time kinetics → interacting mesoscopic plasticity → Hill–Mandel continuum stress → independently calibrated cohesive fracture and material decohesion.

Scientific status: The thesis-supported inputs are 50:50 Wahnström binary Lennard–Jones glasses (1,728 to 1,728,000 atoms in the proposal's preparation summary), typical LSE participation of approximately 1–7 atoms, and sampled barriers approximately 5ε–25ε. All particular three-channel rates, prefactors, mesoscopic event moments, stresses, RVE values, and cohesive laws below are declared illustrative calculations, not measured thesis outputs or validated new simulation results. The figures plot the numerical equations stated here; they are calculated illustrations, not experimental images or MD/FE simulation output.

00 / REFERENCE MODEL AND UNITS

The DFG proposal summarizes a representative event barrier range 5ε–25ε and typical event participation 1–7 atoms. The model uses LJ energy ε, length σLJ and time τ; avoiding an undocumented mapping to physical SI units is essential.

01 / ATOMISTIC → TRANSITION NETWORK

From displacements and potential-energy saddles to unique event descriptors

With one initial minimum A, search and deduplicate first-order saddles Se and connected minima Be. For a channel, compute ΔE‡A→B=U(S)−U(A) and ΔE‡B→A=U(S)−U(B). As a concrete energy accounting example, U(A)=−100ε, U(B)=−102ε, U(S)=−90ε gives forward barrier 10ε and reverse barrier 12ε; the reverse path is essential for state-resolved kinetics.

wᵢ = |Δrᵢ|⁴ / Σⱼ|Δrⱼ|⁴ ; Npart = 1 / Σᵢwᵢ² ; Mxy = ∫Ωe Δγᵖe dV

For four atoms with weighted displacements (0.7,0.1,0.1,0.1), Npart=1/(0.49+0.03)=1.923. This shows why several moving atoms may count as fewer effectively participating atoms. It does not specify the plastic shear moment: that must be estimated separately from the relaxed non-affine displacement field, boundary conditions and an eigenstrain reconstruction.

  • Local event geometry, participation, orientation Q and tensorial transformation moment M, with uncertainty
  • Stress-conditioned barrier derivatives and tested state descriptors

03 / EVENT CLOCK → INTERACTING MESOSCALE ZONES

Transfer tensorial plastic increments and elastic stress changes

Partition an illustrative representative volume into 100 zones of 10σ³ each, so VRVE=1,000σ³. Let each accepted irreversible event contribute integrated engineering plastic shear Mxy=0.16σ³; assign its orientation and local stress redistribution through a periodic elastic Green operator (not explicitly solved in this algebraic demonstration). If 20 net irreversible events occur in the increment, the volume-averaged plastic shear is:

Δγ̄ᵖ = (Σ Mxy,e)/VRVE = 20 × 0.16 / 1000 = 0.0032.

For imposed total shear γ=0.020 and an assumed modulus G=20 ε/σ³, a single averaged elastic-plastic step would give τ=G(γ−γ̄ᵖ)=20(0.02−0.0032)=0.336 ε/σ³. This is a transparent work-level calculation, not proof that a real stochastic RVE would trigger precisely 20 events.

The red curve in Figure 2 is a separately defined surrogate with γ̄ᵖ(γ)=0 for γ≤0.01 and γ̄ᵖ(γ)=0.16(γ−0.01) otherwise. At γ=0.02 it yields γ̄ᵖ=0.0016 and τ=0.368, so it must not be confused with the 20-event snapshot at 0.336.

For an actual simulation, use model A (independent events), B (events + elastic coupling), and C (coupling + post-event structural renewal) on the same prepared glass ensemble. Event-triggered avalanches arise only if the updated neighboring rates and elastic kernels make them occur; they cannot be assumed from a single atomic LSE.

↑ Passed to the next scale

  • Region-level current stress σₐ, plastic strain tensor εᵖₐ, state descriptors zₐ and event rate set
  • Tensorial event moments/orientations and state-dependent elastic interaction field
  • Spatial correlations, irreversible event counts, and dissipation histories

× Not passed / cannot be inferred

04 / MESOSCALE RVE → CONTINUUM CONSTITUTIVE RESPONSE

Work consistency is necessary, but self-averaging must also be demonstrated

Suppose four independently prepared illustrative mesoscale RVEs under the same strain state return τ=(0.30,0.34,0.36,0.32) ε/σ³. The ensemble mean is 0.33 ε/σ³; population standard deviation is 0.02236 ε/σ³. Four RVEs alone are inadequate to establish a representative-volume limit, but they illustrate how variability is passed forward.

P̄ = V⁻¹∫ΩPmdV;   P̄:δF = V⁻¹∫ΩPm:δFmdV (Hill–Mandel).

For simple small-strain shear with an imposed uniform increment δγ=0.005, the calculated average incremental work density is τ̄δγ=0.33×0.005=0.00165 ε/σ³. The stress averaging and work identity must be verified in each RVE boundary-value solve; an ensemble-average arithmetic example does not verify the finite-strain Hill–Mandel condition.

05 / CONTINUUM LOCALIZATION → MATERIAL DECOHESION

A separate, independently calibrated cohesive law is essential

Plastic shear accumulation can identify a potential localization zone, but it does not by itself provide a material separation law. To demonstrate fracture, assume independently calibrated Mode-I traction–separation parameters Tmax=0.4 ε/σ³, δ₀=0.2σ, δf=2σ. A bilinear law rises elastically to peak at δ₀, then linearly softens to zero at δf.

T(δ)= (Tmax/δ₀)δ,   0≤δ≤δ₀;   T(δ)=Tmax(δf−δ)/(δf−δ₀),   δ₀<δ≤δf.

Initial cohesive stiffness Kn=Tmax/δ₀=2 ε/σ⁴. At δ=1σ, T=0.4(2−1)/(2−0.2)=0.222222 ε/σ³. Writing T=(1−d)Knδ gives d=0.888889 at that opening; enforce damage irreversibility during unloading.

Gc = ∫₀δf T(δ)dδ = ½Tmaxδf = ½×0.4×2 = 0.4 ε/σ².

The cumulative work of separation at δ=1σ is the elastic triangle 0.5×0.4×0.2=0.04 plus the softening trapezoid (0.4+0.222222)×(1−0.2)/2=0.248889, totaling 0.288889 ε/σ². Remaining work until full separation is 0.111111 ε/σ². Note that total cohesive work includes recoverable stored energy; irreversible dissipation should be computed separately under the adopted unload/reload damage evolution.

For an illustrative cohesive surface of area A=100σ², complete Mode-I decohesion consumes A Gc=40ε of cohesive work. This is a fully calculated result of the stated toy law, not evidence that the glass simulated in the thesis would fracture across this area. Coupling to a continuum FE or phase-field model would additionally require crack geometry, consistent discretization, loading, boundary conditions and a mesh-objective dissipation/length-scale analysis.

↑ Passed to the next scale

  • From continuum to crack solver: local F or strain, traction, plastic work, stress concentration and potential localization directions
  • Independently measured cohesive strength, traction–opening curve, mixed-mode toughness, and length-scale / mesh regularization
  • Degradation history d and separation work, plus checks against fracture experiments or atomistic separation simulations

× Not passed / cannot be inferred

  • A claimed universal fracture toughness from ARTn barriers or participation counts
  • An assumption that any shear band is automatically a free crack surface
  • An uncalibrated conversion from LJ ε/σ² to physical J/m²
Critical scientific boundary: The thesis supports local rearrangements and barrier/stress trends; it does not provide the cohesive traction law, mixed-mode fracture calibration, macroscopic crack path or validation needed to predict actual material decohesion.
