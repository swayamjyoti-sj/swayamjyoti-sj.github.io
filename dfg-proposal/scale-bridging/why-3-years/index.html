07 / SCIENTIFIC RATIONALE AND 36-MONTH RESEARCH PROGRAMME

Why 3 years?

Because a numerical demonstration is not a validated prediction. The examples above calculate the consequences of selected model parameters. The 36-month DFG project must determine whether these parameters, rate laws and cross-scale transfer rules can be obtained from real atomistic ensembles, remain statistically reliable, and predict independent outcomes without refitting.

What the website already establishes

  • Conservation, event-rate and work-average equations can be evaluated with specified input parameters.
  • Barrier populations generate different conditional event probabilities and waiting times.
  • Plastic event moments can be mapped into averaged plastic strain, under declared assumptions.
  • An independently prescribed cohesive law permits computed traction, damage and separation energy.

What the examples do not establish

  • Converged, state-conditioned barrier networks and physical prefactors from actual glasses.
  • Whether thermally activated ARTn channels predict stress-driven AQS instability sites.
  • Whether interacting events generate real avalanche statistics or persistent bands.
  • Whether a local, deterministic continuum closure is transferable across samples and load paths.
  • Whether or when plastic localization leads to measured material decohesion.

The 36-month sequence

Year 1 · M1–M12Reproducible atomistic reference states; ARTn saddle catalogues, reverse checks, stress dependence; initial matched AQS tests and uncertainty bounds
Year 2 · M13–M24Validate rate preservation and thermal–athermal correspondence; simulate independent, elastically coupled and renewed mesoscale event networks; predict avalanche statistics
Year 3 · M25–M36Test finite-strain work consistency, RVE convergence, held-out continuum predictions and localization; evaluate damage/fracture only with separate separation evidence

Work packages overlap. The proposal schedules WP1 for M1–M12, WP2 for M8–M20 and additional mesoscale, continuum and validation work throughout later phases. The three-year sequence above is a synthesis, not a replacement for the detailed work-package schedule.

The decisive falsifiable results

Test 1 — Atomistic-to-athermal correspondence. Compare independent ARTn rate-weighted susceptibility maps with AQS event locations and orientations on matched, held-out glass configurations. Better-than-random agreement supports shared susceptibility; systematic disagreement establishes distinct event-selection mechanisms.

Test 2 — Does coarse-graining preserve kinetics? Increase independent ARTn searches until the cumulative influential escape rate and predicted first-event waiting-time quantiles stabilize, or report quantitative bounds for missing channels. A stable barrier histogram alone is insufficient.

Test 3 — When does a continuum law work? Demonstrate Hill–Mandel consistency separately from statistical representativeness. Compare deterministic, stochastic and nonlocal closures using identical calibration information, independently prepared RVEs and withheld loading histories. A closure that fails out-of-sample reveals a physical limit of information averaging.

Test 4 — Does shear imply separation? Distinguish transient avalanches from persistent plastic bands, and both from cohesive damage. Do not assert a crack path or fracture toughness from atomic barriers alone; assess fracture only after independent separation calibration.

Central scientific value: determine precisely which information must survive the atomistic → kinetics → mesoscale → continuum steps to predict collective plasticity, which information can safely be discarded, and where the predictive mapping fails. A negative, statistically rigorous transfer test can be just as important as a successful one.

Bottom line. The webpage answers: “What would the equations calculate if the parameters were known?” The DFG project answers: “Can those parameters and transfer laws be measured and validated, and do they predict unseen glasses and load histories?” That requires substantial new simulation ensembles, uncertainty analysis and scientific falsification—not merely running the illustrative formulae at larger scale.

Grounding: DFG proposal, especially §§1.3, 2.2–2.3, 2.6–2.12 and the WP/validation programme. Thesis-derived scope is limited to completed atomistic LSE characterization; the cross-scale predictive tests are proposed research, not doctoral-thesis outcomes.
