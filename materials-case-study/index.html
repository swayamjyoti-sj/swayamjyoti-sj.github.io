RESEARCH CASE STUDY · MODEL GLASSES

How do atomic rearrangements respond to load?

A material’s response begins with small changes in atomic configuration. Studying those events can help formulate more detailed models of deformation.

The scientific question

Which local rearrangements are accessible in a model glass, and how does loading change their activation barriers?

Published evidence

Our 2014 study used ARTn to characterize accessible rearrangements in model Lennard–Jones glasses. It identified extended and ring-shaped chains. Correlations with local structural and elastic properties were weak; events occurred throughout the sample.

Our 2016 study examined how loading changes these events using activation-relaxation and nudged elastic band methods. It connected the response of barrier energies to the events’ internal stress patterns.

2014 paper: Physical Review B 89, 224201
2016 paper: Physical Review B 93, 144202
2018 erratum to the 2016 paper

Why this matters for a collaboration

It motivates a specific test: can event-level information improve predictions beyond a chosen baseline for a defined material and loading path?

Proposed next study

  1. Build an event catalogue for agreed reference states.
  2. Check barriers and loading responses against independent calculations.
  3. Evaluate a scale-transfer model on held-out states and loading histories.
  4. Report predictive errors, computational cost and failure cases.

The published studies concern model glasses. Predicting engineering-component lifetimes or fracture in commercial alloys requires further validation; those capabilities are not established by these papers.
